Metal casting enables the production of components with complex geometries, varied dimensions, and application- specific material requirements. Selecting the appropriate casting process depends on component design, alloy, production volume, and required quality.

01 / CASTING TECHNOLOGIES

Our Casting Processes

Different casting methods provide different advantages in tooling, geometry, dimensional capability, and production scale. The following processes cover a broad range of metal component requirements.

Sand Casting

Sand casting forms metal components by pouring molten metal into a sand-based mould. It is adaptable to a wide range of component sizes and designs.

  • Steel components
  • Aluminium components
  • Complex shapes and varied component sizes

Pressure Die Casting

Pressure die casting injects molten metal into a reusable metal die under pressure. It is commonly used for repeatable production of accurately shaped components.

  • Suitable aluminium and other compatible alloys
  • Repeatable component geometry
  • High-volume production applications

Centrifugal Casting

Centrifugal casting uses rotation to distribute molten metal inside a mould. The process is particularly suited to many cylindrical and rotationally symmetrical components.

  • Selected cylindrical components
  • Rings, sleeves, and tubes where suitable
  • Process-dependent material and size options

Investment Casting

Investment casting uses a precision wax pattern and ceramic shell mould to produce intricate metal components with detailed features.

  • Complex component geometries
  • Fine details and intricate features
  • Suitable alloys based on application needs

Engineering Applications

Cast components are used across industrial machinery, mechanical assemblies, equipment housings, structural parts, and specialized engineering systems. Process selection should account for material specification, dimensional tolerances, surface finish, operating conditions, and inspection requirements.
